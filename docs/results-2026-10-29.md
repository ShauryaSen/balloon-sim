# Results, 2026-10-29: the sweep on a task with room in it

The fourth sweep, and the first on a task chosen rather than inherited. 7,904 cells, all completed, no
failures. Rows on Unicorn in `~/balloon-sim/results/rows-2026-10-24/`; tables from `scripts/summarise.py`,
one run per arm, in `results/summary-2026-10-24.txt`; figures in `figures/2026-10-24/`.

Read it against `results-2026-10-22.md`, the 1,000 km sweep. Three things changed between them: the episode
ends when the balloon arrives, the goal moved to 2,000 km, and there is a fourth controller.

## Setup

Every number here is in km, summed over the moves of the episode, which is what the objective is and what
the figures now draw. An earlier version of this file divided them by the 50-move budget and called the
result an average distance; that was a constant rescale, so nothing about any comparison changed, but under
the arrival rule a flight that arrived on move 17 had its cost spread over 33 moves it never flew.
`results/summary-2026-10-24-*.txt` is regenerated from the rows and is the authority for any of them.

Global ERA5, 2021-07-16, 3 h moves, six levels 14.8 to 20.2 km. Launches 20N to 50N, **goal 2,000 km
downwind**, 50 moves, the episode ending the first time the balloon is within 200 km and the moves after it
costing nothing (DESIGN 37). Belief: a GP with the forecast as its mean and a kernel fitted to a day-old
forecast's error in the band (3.2 m/s, 544 km, 1.3 km, 9.1 h). Noise sigma = {0, ¼, ½, 1, 2, 4} × 3.2 m/s.
32 paired seeds: within a seed, every controller and every noise level fly the same start, goal and true
wind, and the error field is one pattern scaled by sigma.

Four controllers. **hindsight** replans each move on the true wind, and is reported as the best search any
clairvoyant cell found. **fixed_plan** plans the whole episode once on belief draws and flies blind.
**mpc** replans every move and conditions its belief on the wind it reads at its own position. **mpc_blind**
replans every move and never looks at its readings.

The task comes from `scouting-rule.md`: of twenty candidates, 2,000 km downwind was the only one both
reachable and not free. Hindsight arrives on 25 of 32 seeds at a median move 20 of 50, so an episode holds
about twenty moves of decisions.

## 1. Replanning is worth 16,000 to 29,000 km. Learning from the balloon's own wind readings is worth nothing.

This is the result the sweep was run for, and it is the cleaner of the two answers it could have given.

MPC minus the same controller with its readings withheld, on the belief's mean, distance to the goal
summed over the moves, paired over 32 seeds:

| σ | 0 | 0.8 | 1.6 | 3.2 | 6.4 | 12.8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| what the readings were worth, horizon 20 | 0 | −250 | +294 | +59 | −985 | −2,249 |

Not one of the 24 comparisons across four horizons and six noise levels shows a detectable benefit. The one
that clears zero (σ=0.8 at horizon 50, +1,295 km [+45, +2,546]) points the wrong way, and at 24 tests one hit is
what chance gives. The same holds at 1,000 km and at 3,000 km, so it replicates across three tasks.

What the gap over a blind plan is instead, with the readings withheld (so this is replanning alone):

| σ | 0 | 0.8 | 1.6 | 3.2 | 6.4 | 12.8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| horizon 20 | −16,358 | −17,182 | −21,233 | −28,524 | −23,481 | −21,618 |

Every one of those excludes zero, on 24 to 30 seeds of 32. Full MPC, with the readings, gives −16,350,
−17,450, −20,950, −28,450, −24,450, −23,850: the same numbers.

At σ=0 the readings are worth exactly zero on every seed, to the last digit, on 0 of 32. That is the harness
checking itself: with a perfect forecast the belief is already exact, so conditioning on a reading cannot
move it, and `mpc_blind` differs from `mpc` in nothing else.

Why so little? The kernel's horizontal lengthscale is 544 km and the balloon reads one point per move. Twenty
readings strung along a single line say almost nothing about a field it is planning across for thousands of
kilometres. The anemometer is not useless in principle; it is useless at this sampling rate against this
lengthscale.

## 2. The breaking point moved in, to about half a real forecast error

Each horizon minus the next longer one, MPC on the mean, summed distance, paired:

| σ | H5 − H10 | H10 − H20 | H20 − H50 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **+2,558** [+392, +4,724] | **+2,407** [+1,132, +3,681] | +1,204 [−131, +2,539] |
| 0.8 | +2,411 [−2, +4,824] | **+2,261** [+1,187, +3,335] | −167 [−1,578, +1,244] |
| 1.6 | **+1,270** [+238, +2,302] | **+2,132** [+627, +3,636] | +410 [−845, +1,666] |
| 3.2 | +2,604 [−1,260, +6,469] | −298 [−1,976, +1,380] | +1,735 [−325, +3,795] |
| 6.4 | −8 [−1,978, +1,963] | −670 [−3,021, +1,681] | −171 [−3,113, +2,771] |
| 12.8 | +1,755 [−3,166, +6,676] | −3,283 [−9,716, +3,150] | +2,915 [−1,749, +7,580] |

A longer horizon pays through σ=1.6 and nothing is detectable from σ=3.2 on. The 1,000 km sweep put the break
between 3.2 and 6.4; on a task that requires steering it sits between **1.6 and 3.2 m/s**, half to one times
a real day-old forecast error. Harder task, lookahead stops paying sooner.

Going past horizon 20 was never detectably better at any noise level, here or on the 1,000 km sweep. Sixty
hours is as far ahead as it is worth planning.

## 3. Hedging over belief draws helps a controller that cannot react, and not one that can

The fixed plan, most draws against fewest, summed distance:

| arm | σ=0.8 | σ=1.6 | σ=3.2 | σ=6.4 | σ=12.8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| calibrated, 64 vs mean | **−8,403** | **−8,722** | **−11,941** | −1,956 | −2,278 |
| belief 2× too wide, 16 vs mean | **−8,300** | **−7,750** | **−16,550** | +2,950 | −8,400 |
| belief 0.5× too narrow, 16 vs mean | −1,700 | −6,850 | **−14,850** | −3,550 | −2,450 |
| nested, 16 vs 1 draw | — | — | **−14,600** | −767 | −5,932 |

Bold excludes zero. For MPC the same comparison is undetectable in every cell of every arm — 24 of 24 in the
main arm, and nothing in any other.

On the 1,000 km sweep no fixed-plan interval cleared zero at all. The effect appearing now is the
sample-average bound finally doing something, on a task where committing to a plan actually costs you.

**The obvious explanation for the MPC half is wrong.** We expected the draws to do nothing for MPC because
the belief is calibrated by construction, noise and belief sharing a kernel, so its mean is already
sufficient. The calibration arm tests that directly, and the pattern does not move: with a belief that is
twice or half as wide as the truth, draws still help the fixed plan at low and moderate noise and still do
nothing for MPC. So the reason is the simpler one. **A controller that replans corrects errors as they
appear, and hedging against them in advance is redundant. A controller that cannot react has only hedging.**

The nested arm rules out the other explanation, that the effect is which winds a run happened to draw rather
than how many. With the draws held fixed as subsets of one pool, the fixed plan improves monotonically with
the count — 35,650, 30,950, 21,050 km at σ=3.2 across 1, 4 and 16 draws — and MPC does not: 7,650, 7,850,
6,650.

## 4. The bounds hold, except where a short horizon makes MPC myopic

On the objective, km above hindsight, σ=0: the fixed plan is 962, MPC at horizon 5 is 8,929, at 10 is
6,229, at 20 is 3,047, at 50 is 391. With a perfect forecast a blind plan over the whole episode beats a controller that
replans but only looks five moves ahead. Myopia costs more than a wrong forecast does when the forecast is
right. From σ=0.8 on, every MPC horizon is below the fixed plan.

## 5. The two metrics disagree at low noise, and both are right

At σ=0 the fixed plan is 962 km above hindsight on the objective and 18,838 km above on summed distance.
MPC at horizon 20 is 3,047 and 2,500. The blind plan reaches the goal about as fast as anyone, so the arrival rule stops
counting before its plan goes wrong; then it flies off, which only the summed metric sees. MPC stays near
the goal, which the arrival rule does not reward.

Neither is a mistake. The objective asks "how well did it get there", the summed metric asks "where was it
over six days". Anything claimed from one should say which.

## The other distances

| | 1,000 km | 2,000 km | 3,000 km |
|---|---|---|---|
| hindsight arrives | 29/32, median move 8 | 25/32, move 20 | 17/32, move 25 |
| replanning, σ=3.2 | −1036 | −569 | −429 |
| replanning, σ=0 | −752 | −327 | −69, not detectable |

The ordering and the value of replanning survive at both, so the result is not an artefact of the chosen
distance. The effect is largest at 1,000 km on summed distance, because a blind plan there arrives early and
then has forty moves to be blown away — which is the free-ride problem the arrival rule was introduced for,
showing up in the metric that ignores it.

## Caveats

- Hindsight is the best plan our searches found on the true wind, not the true optimum.
- One ERA5 window, 2021-07-16, ten days, one season. Everything here is about that window until another runs.
- Within a seed the noise ladder is one error pattern scaled, not six independent draws, so the sigma axis
  carries 32 realisations and not 192.
- The belief's kernel is fitted to this record's own day-old forecast error. The calibration arm varies its
  amplitude but not its shape; a kernel with the wrong lengthscales is untested.
- The arrival radius is 200 km and the episode 50 moves. Both were chosen, not derived.
- Crosswind, upwind and random goals were unreachable at every distance tried (`scouting-rule.md`), so this
  is a downwind result. An altitude-only balloon in this record goes downwind or nowhere.
