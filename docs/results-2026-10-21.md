# Results, 2026-10-21: the noise sweep on global ERA5

The second sweep (the first, on cBottle, is in `cbottle-2026-10-21.md`). Rows are on Unicorn in
`~/balloon-sim/results/rows/`, one file per cell; figures in `figures/` here and there (gitignored); Slurm arrays
452318, 452319, 452378-452380, and 457650 for the 64-draw fixed-plan cells, which ran out of 8 GB and were
rerun at 40 GB.

## Setup (DESIGN.md 30-34)

Global ERA5, 2021-07-16, 3 h moves, six levels 14.8 to 20.2 km. Launches 20N to 50N at the middle altitude,
goal 300 km downwind, 50-move episodes. Belief: GP with the forecast as mean, kernel fitted in the band to a
day-old forecast's error (3.2 m/s, 544 km, 1.3 km, 9.1 h), conditioned on the balloon's own readings each move.
Noise sigma = {0, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4} x 3.2 m/s, the forecast being the truth less a draw from that kernel.
Hindsight replans on the truth over the whole episode; the fixed plan plans once on belief draws and flies
blind; MPC replans every move on fresh draws. 32 seeds, paired: the same truth, start, goal and error pattern
across every controller and noise level within a seed.

## Hindsight, the ceiling

hindsight: avg distance median 413 km (IQR 239-626), final median 480 km, within 200 km 8/32, within 500 km 18/32

So the goal is reached but not held: the calmest level in the column is a median 3 to 5 m/s, which is 40 to 50 km
a move, and five days of it is more than the two-level choice can cancel. Final distance is therefore a weak
metric here; the summed cost (average distance over the episode) is what separates controllers.

## What the tables say

1. **Order.** At every noise level, fixed plan above MPC above hindsight. At sigma 0 the full-horizon MPC is
   hindsight (same controller on the same winds), and the fixed plan is within 50 km of it.
2. **Noise.** Both forecast-based controllers degrade steadily. The fixed plan loses about 100 km of average
   distance per m/s of forecast error; MPC loses about half that.
3. **Horizon, the C3 question.** With a good forecast a longer horizon pays: at sigma 0 the excess is 240 km at
   horizon 5, 173 at 10, 31 at 20, 0 at 50. As the noise grows the horizons converge: at sigma 12.8 every horizon
   sits 590 to 740 km above hindsight, and horizon 50 is no better than 20 from sigma 3.2 on. The point where a
   longer horizon stops recovering performance is between sigma 3 and 6 m/s, one to two times a real day-old
   forecast's error.
4. **Draws.** For MPC, averaging the plan over belief draws does not help at this noise and kernel: 0, 1, 4 and
   16 draws are within seed noise of each other. For the fixed plan, which has no replanning to fall back on,
   draws do help: 64 draws beat the mean by 100 to 130 km of average distance at every noise level above zero,
   and 16 by about 100. That is the sample-average bound of the write-up, and it says hedging matters when you
   cannot react, and matters little when you can.
5. **Score.** MPC closes roughly half the gap between a blind plan and knowing the wind, at every noise level
   (median over seeds about 0.5, quartiles 0.2 to 0.8). It does not fall toward zero as the noise grows, so the
   forecast, not the controller, is what degrades: the interpretation Sarah laid out on 2026-10-09.

## Caveats to say out loud

- Hindsight is the best plan our optimiser found, not the true optimum (DESIGN.md 34).
- The goal is reached but not held; a shorter episode or a station-keeping cost would change the picture.
- The belief is calibrated by construction (noise and belief share a kernel); a mismatched belief is the
  next experiment.
- Seeds with a seed-level draw of the error pattern scaled by sigma: the noise ladder within a seed is one
  pattern growing, not six independent draws.
